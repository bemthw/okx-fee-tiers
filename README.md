## The published rate is not the rate you pay

CryptoSlate makes the point plainly: the lowest published maker tier may require volume you will never reach, and a zero-fee pair can carry a wider spread or different execution rules that give the cost back. A fee table becomes useful only once you know your eligible tier and the pair you intend to trade.

OKX's own fee documentation describes tiers set by assets under management and 30-day volume, updated daily, with the steepest discounts reserved for very large volume. Its worked example describes a user with more than $125 million in 30-day volume receiving a -0.0075% maker rate and 0.0175% taker. That is a real tier. It is also irrelevant to someone trading $20,000 a month.

Region changes the number more than volume does at the entry level. A November 2026 fee breakdown puts the global regular-user spot rate at 0.08% maker and 0.10% taker, the EEA site at 0.08%/0.10% in EUR, the US entity at 0.20% maker and 0.35% taker, and the UAE site at 0.40%/0.60%. On a $1,000 buy-and-sell round trip, that is roughly $2.00 of platform fees on the global schedule versus about $7.00 on the US schedule. The same brand, the same trade, more than triple the cost.

Entry point matters just as much as region. Kraken is the cleanest illustration: its app's simple buy flow adds 1% to 1.5% plus spread, while Kraken Pro starts at 0.10% maker/taker. A day trader using the simple screen is paying ten times the professional rate for the same underlying order. Every exchange with both a "buy crypto" button and an advanced terminal has some version of this gap.

Then add the costs that never appear in a maker/taker column:

- **Funding payments** on perpetuals, which can exceed your fee spend if you hold a crowded position through a funding window.
- **Conversion costs** when your deposit currency is not the quote currency of the pair you want.
- **Borrowing interest** on margin positions, charged for as long as the position stays open.
- **Withdrawal network fees**, which vary by coin, network and congestion and are charged per withdrawal.
- **Settlement fees** on dated futures contracts, which apply regardless of tier.

A trader who counts only the headline percentage and ignores those five lines is guessing at profitability.

## Liquidity is a property of the pair, not the exchange

Headline volume tells you nothing about the depth available in the pair on your screen. Check the bid, the offer, the spread and the nearby book levels, then estimate the fill you would get at your intended size. Repeat the check during a stressed session, because a liquid major does not make every altcoin liquid.

OKX's own material on intraday trading ranks liquidity first among selection criteria, and its ordering of the majors is a fair starting point. Bitcoin has the deepest book and tightest spread, so large size moves the price least. Ethereum is second on liquidity but swings more within a day. Solana is high beta, with 10% to 20% daily moves that are not unusual, and it belongs to traders who have already learned how to size a stop. Small-cap tokens are the other direction entirely: thin liquidity, wide spreads, and a market structure that makes pump-and-dump schemes easy.

Order handling deserves its own check because it is where real money leaks. Stop, stop-limit, take-profit, reduce-only, post-only, IOC and FOK orders are not standardized across venues, and their behaviour during a gap can differ from what the label suggests. You need to know whether cancelling a remaining limit order leaves an already-filled position open, and how a conditional order triggers when price jumps past the trigger. Send a small order through each order type you plan to use before you rely on it.

Leverage is the constraint most often missed. Finder states that no major exchange based in Australia can offer margin to Australian users because of legal restrictions, so a strategy built on 5x spot exposure has to be rebuilt or moved to a venue outside that regime.

Before you compare fees, confirm three things about the entity that will hold your money: which products it may legally offer you, what deposit and withdrawal rails it supports in your currency, and which regulator it answers to.

## Who should not day trade crypto at all

Finder puts the loss rate at roughly 95% of traders. OKX's own educational material says the vast majority of people who attempt day trading lose money and that beginners should avoid leverage. Those two statements come from opposite sides of the industry and agree.

If a 20% drawdown in a week would change your plans, this is the wrong activity and the ranking above is irrelevant to you. If you plan to hold for years, paying taker fees hundreds of times a month works against you. Leverage amplifies losses with the same efficiency as gains, and on small-cap pairs the spread can exceed the move you were trying to capture.

Custody risk sits on top of market risk. Australian exchanges are registered with AUSTRAC but still lack mandatory insurance or strict rules on how user assets are managed. Accounts can be frozen without warning, which is why keeping only a trading balance on an exchange is the common precaution. "Not your keys, not your crypto" remains accurate.

## FAQ

**Should I use spot or perpetuals for day trading?**
Perpetuals let you short and apply leverage, and they charge funding on top of fees. Spot has no liquidation risk and no funding, but you can only profit from rising prices unless you add margin. Match the instrument to the strategy rather than to the fee table.

**How much capital do I need?**
No exchange in this comparison publishes a minimum that matters. What matters is whether your fees and spreads are small relative to your expected edge. At the global regular spot rate, a $1,000 round trip costs about $2.00 before spread; if your average target is smaller than that plus the spread, the strategy loses before it starts.

**Why do two sources list different OKX fees?**
Because they measured different things. OKX runs separate entities for different regions with separate schedules, and rates change with 30-day volume and assets. A published 0.08%/0.10% and a published 0.70%/0.70% can both be real numbers from different entities or different measurement dates. The fee page inside your own account is the only figure that binds.

**Can US traders use OKX's futures and options?**
CryptoSlate's snapshot of the US entity lists spot and a simple-buy broker, not the full derivatives suite. The US spot schedule also differs from the global one, at 0.20% maker and 0.35% taker for a regular user.

**Do more listed coins mean better day trading?**
Rarely. Kraken's own guidance says deep liquidity on major pairs can matter more to a day trader than access to thousands of obscure altcoins. A 3,000-instrument venue and a 300-instrument venue can offer identical execution on BTC/USDT.
